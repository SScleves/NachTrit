Nachtrit one-hour review

What this is. Nachtrit is a game that teaches Dutch to English speakers. A player cycles through a Dutch town at night and picks the right word at forks in the road. All the Dutch below was written without a native speaker.

What we ask.

  1. Read each Dutch line. The underlined words are what the player has to choose. The English below it is what the player sees as the meaning.
  2. Tick one box: natural (a Dutch speaker would say or write this), odd but correct (grammatical, but nobody says it like that), or wrong.
  3. Under some lines you see the wrong options the player can pick. If one of them is also correct Dutch in that sentence, please write it in the notes. That is the most useful thing you can tell us.
  4. Use the notes for a better way to say it. A few words are enough.

Netherlands Dutch is the target. Where a level uses dialect (Urk, Groningen, Maastricht) it says so. You can tick on paper, or on screen and press Save my marks as a file and send us that file.

Both sentences talk about yesterday, and the English is past. The game marks the present tense (eten we, duurt) as wrong. In a story ("Zit ik gisteren in de trein...") the present is possible. As a plain statement, is the present wrong enough to be a wrong option?

4. The Zeeland town text
On the map, this joke comes right after the line about the 1,836 people who died in 1953. It can read as the drowned knocking on the barrier. Would a Zeeuw find this in bad taste?
town-zeeland
In 1953 kwam de zee 's nachts binnen en vielen er 1.836 doden. Nu staan er deuren in de zee, en die gaan dicht als het stormt.
De kering heeft 62 deuren. Bij storm gaan ze allemaal dicht. Er klopt toch iemand.
In 1953 the sea came in at night and 1,836 people died. Now there are doors in the sea, and they close when it storms. The barrier has 62 doors. On storm nights they all close. Someone still knocks.

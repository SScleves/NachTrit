# Nachtrit: the story

## The premise

For years, every time someone tried a word of Dutch, the Dutch switched to English. One night the
language got tired of being ignored and left in the mist. Without it people forgot how to talk;
only the habits stayed: splitting the bill, being tall, checking the Tikkie. The dead are the words
that got lost. The only one who can bring Dutch back is the one man who never learned it.

## The fix (what the player does)

- Every passenger delivered in correct Dutch is a word given back. Wrong Dutch: the word stays
  lost, and it pays nothing.
- When a town's places are delivered, the mist lifts there: its windows light up on the map, dawn
  comes, and its zombies turn back into people (still splitting the bill, now in Dutch).
- Amsterdam is the last town. When it is saved, the language comes home.

## Nobody agrees what caused it (running gag)

Never settled. Each town has its own theory; one rumour per town card, level intro or loading line.

## Editor's notes

Dutch, intro panels

- 1: "de mist kwam" -> "de mist opkwam". Mist "komt op" in Dutch; the bare verb reads as a translation.
- 2: "Tegen de ochtend" -> "De volgende ochtend". Same meaning, the way people say it. Rest kept.
- 3: "Niemand weet wat het veroorzaakte" said "nobody knows", not "nobody agrees"; now "Niemand is het eens over de oorzaak". "het Vlaams het mooiere Nederlands noemde" was a word-for-word comparative that nobody says; now "waarin Vlaams het mooiste Nederlands werd genoemd". "Of aan de miljoenste toerist" keeps the "schuld geven aan" construction running. "Waarschijnlijk alles tegelijk" -> "Waarschijnlijk allemaal", drier.
- 4: "Elke keer dat iemand het probeerde" was vague about what "het" is; "Elke keer als iemand een poging deed" is the spoken form. "vertrok hij" -> "is hij vertrokken" (perfect tense is what you say out loud; the simple past sounds written).
- 5: "hij oefende nooit" -> "nooit geoefend", same reason. "was er zo aardig over" is not an idiom; "deed er zo aardig over" is.
- 6: the original had the whole subject clause before the verb, grammatical but heavy. Reordered to "Nu begrijpen alleen twee honden hem nog, en een grammaticaboek...", which also lands the book as an afterthought, where the joke is.
- 7: "Bladzijde" -> "Pagina". Both correct; "pagina" is what people say.
- 8: kept.
- 10: "enthousiast lijken" -> "enthousiast overkomen". "Overkomen" is the verb for how you come across to others, which is the point of the joke.
- 11: kept. "Fak" is how it is spelled in Dutch chat, so it is right.

Dutch, rumours

- Alblasserdam: Tikkie is a het-word (it is a diminutive, tikje), so "dat", not "die". Also swapped "never paid" for "still open" ("staat nog open" is the wording the app itself uses). Bonus: the first rumour is now a de/het example, which fits panel 7.
- Kinderdijk: "vroeg of de molens even stil wilden staan" is the spoken polite form; the "om te" version is correct but stiff.
- Zeeland: "Weer iemand die..." is how a Dutch person would sigh it. English got the "Again." as its own sentence to match the rhythm.
- Utrecht: "maar dan ironisch" is the standard Dutch way to add "but ironically"; the bare adverb at the end did not read as a joke.
- Den Haag: "De Kamer debatteert" is newspaper Dutch. "De Tweede Kamer vergadert er nog over" is the national joke about The Hague, that they are always in a meeting. English adjusted to "still in a meeting about it" so the reference matches.
- Rotterdam: the Dutch now ends on the Rotterdam motto "Niet lullen maar poetsen" (less talking, more scrubbing), which every Dutch reader knows and which says "They are working" better than a literal line. English line kept as the meaning reference.
- Urk: added "Op Urk" (Dutch say "op Urk", it used to be an island; locals will notice). "niets" -> "niks" for speech.
- Texel: "weten ervan" and "zeggen niks" are the spoken forms.
- Amsterdam: kept. "Dronken Duitser" is a real thing Dutch people hear about their own language, so it lands.

Jokes flagged and replaced (two replacements, under the limit of three)

- Groningen, "The earth shook. Again." Replaced. The gas quakes are an ongoing real harm: tens of thousands of damaged houses, people waiting years for repairs, a parliamentary inquiry that called it a disgrace. A Groninger would not read this as ribbing. New rumour uses the one Groningen stereotype Groningers tell about themselves: the highest compliment there is "kon minder" (could be worse). "Ze vroegen Groningen wat er gebeurd was. Groningen zei: kon minder."
- Maastricht, "Mayonnaise on the fries, in a Belgian way." Replaced, not because it is mean but because it does not land: the Dutch also put mayonnaise on fries, so a Dutch reader does not see the joke, and Belgian jokes as a genre are dated. New rumour is a language joke, which fits the game: the friet/patat divide. Below the rivers it is "friet", in the Randstad "patat", and both sides pretend to be offended. "Iemand bestelde patat in Maastricht. Dat heet hier friet. Daar is nog niemand overheen."
- Urk, kept. Sunday rest is the gentle Urk joke, and Urkers make it themselves. The ones to avoid would be anything about the riots, drugs or the fishing-quota anger; Sunday is safe.
- Flemish being the nicer Dutch, kept. Dutch people say this themselves (Flemish is "zachter"), so it is self-ribbing, not a dig at Belgium.
- Zeeland and Holland, kept. Mild. Note that the provinces most touchy about "Holland" are Friesland, Brabant and Limburg; Zeeland works but any of those would land harder if a town moves.

Not touched

- The premise and fix sections are English only; left as they were.
- Panel 9 ("...") left empty as it was.
- No exclamation marks anywhere, as per voice.

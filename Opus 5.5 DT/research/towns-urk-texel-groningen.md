# NachTrit art direction research: Urk, Texel and Groningen

The three places look and feel different at night:

* **Urk** is a lit hill rising out of a black polder next to a black lake. Its white lighthouse turns counterclockwise. It is a very churchgoing fishing village, and its traditional dress comes out in large numbers on Urkerdag.
* **Texel** is close to fully dark. A red lighthouse flashes twice every 10 seconds over dunes, sheep sheds and turf walls. The sky is roughly Bortle 4 to 5, and the aurora was seen there in 2024 and 2026.
* **Groningen** is a bright city of canals and bikes. The Martinitoren is now lit warm at the foot and cooler at the top, and cloudy nights glow because the clouds reflect the city's light.

Numbers in brackets point to the source list under each place. Anything I could not verify is marked as unsourced or uncertain. Coming dates after today (27 October 2026): Sint Maarten on 11 November, Urk in Wintersferen on 27 November, Ouwe Sunderklaas on 12 December and WinterWelVaart from 11 to 13 December.

## 1. Urk

**Essence.** A former island: a little hill in a flat polder, with a white lighthouse, a church by the water and the traditional costume on high days [1][7][10][21].

**Ground and topology.**
* The old island is a boulder clay hill up to about 9 m above NAP [1].
* The polder around it lies about 3 to 4 m below NAP [2].
* The IJsselmeer is kept 0.2 to 0.4 m below NAP [3].
* At night this reads as a lit mound above black water, with black fields lying even lower.

**The ride.**
* The way in is the IJsselmeer dike from Lemmer [4].
* It passes 86 wind turbines up to 198.5 m tall; 48 of them stand in the lake [5].
* The road then climbs into narrow alleys called ginkies, little squares and the harbour [6].
* Since 2021 a row of posts traces the old island shoreline [1].

**Skyline and landmarks.**
* **Lighthouse.** A round white brick tower, 18.5 m tall, with its light 27 m above NAP. A copper dome sits on a red base [7][8][9].
  * It gives one short white flash every 5 s [7][8][9].
  * It is the only Dutch lighthouse whose light turns counterclockwise [7][8][9].
* **Kerkje aan de Zee (1786).** Brick, with round arched windows, a tower with an onion top (since 1954), a bell from 1456 and a churchyard [10][11][12].
* **Fishermen's monument (1968).** A fisherman's wife glancing back at the sea, above 379 names in marble [13].
* **Ommelebommelestien.** A granite boulder 30 m offshore. On the quay stands a 1998 statue of a father and midwife in their boat [4].

**Buildings.**
* The old core has been protected since 2007 [14].
* It is mostly small 19th century fishermen's houses with plastered fronts, saddle roofs and brick pointed gables [15].
* Addresses look like "Wijk 3 80" (that is the lighthouse) [15].
* Houses have white and green fences, with anchors and little ships as ornament [16].

**Street furniture and light.** I found no source on Urk's street lamp types. During Urk in Wintersferen the old village is lit by torches, lanterns and fire baskets [17].

**Sky.**
* There is no local measurement and no dark sky park nearby.
* The nearest station in the University of Groningen measurement network, Weerribben (about 27 km away), reads 20.6 to 20.8 mag/arcsec² [18]. That is roughly Bortle 5 [19].
* Treat this as a proxy only.

**People.**
* **Men's dress** [20]:
  * wide black pleated breeches closed with two domed silver buttons showing Bible scenes
  * a collarless jacket worn open over a red and white striped vest, with a red bib and gold studs
  * a silk neckerchief threaded through a gold ring
  * an old style sailor's cap or a low black hat
* **Women's dress** [21][22][23]:
  * a corset covered in blue damask
  * a colourful silk kraplap (a decorated bodice front) that the wearer embroiders herself, with a black fringed cloth over it on Sundays
  * a hul (cap) with a yellow lace edge, worn over a narrow silver oorijzer (a metal head band)
  * a striped apron, black on Sundays
  * several strands of glass garnets
* **Who wears it.** In 2003 only 20 women and 8 men wore it every day [21]. Today it is mostly worn by choirs, at baptisms and some weddings. On Urkerdag, the Saturday before Whitsun, most of the village wears it [22][20]. Show it as festive heritage worn with pride.
* **Church life.** About 98% of residents belong to one of some twenty churches [1]. On Sundays the shops stay shut and families walk along the dike and the harbour [24].
* **Fishing.** Urk has by far the largest fishing fleet in the Netherlands, and its boats carry UK numbers [1][25].
* **Dialect.** Urkers drops the h. "Tate" means father and "bessien" grandmother [26]. I found no reliable source for a typical greeting.

**Sounds.** The 1456 church bell [10] and the male choirs [1]. Gulls are plausible but I found no source.

**Night moments and folklore.**
* Urk in Wintersferen is on the evening of Friday 27 November 2026 [17].
* In local folklore, babies come from the Ommelebommelestien. The father and the midwife row out and buy the child from the beings under the stone: one guilder for a girl, two for a boy [4].
* Asking a man whether he has "been to Schokland" means asking whether he has children [1].

**Humour hooks.**
* A lighthouse that turns the wrong way [7].
* The test phrase "Un kweeuwkien mit gerespelde keaze" (a rusk with grated cheese), used to check whether someone can really speak Urkers [26].
* The highest birth rate in the country [1].
* The biggest Dutch town with no bus on Sundays [1].

## 2. Texel

**Essence.** A red lighthouse at the end of the land, sheep beside half roofed sheds, and a sky darker than on the mainland [3][16][11].

**Ground and topology.**
* A glacial core, the Hoge Berg, rises to 15 m above NAP. Its fields are boxed in by turf walls [13].
* On the sea side the dunes reach 24.8 m, and the tide floods the dune valley De Slufter [1].
* Flat polders lie to the north and east behind the Wadden dike. Den Burg sits at about 2 m [1][2].
* At night: dune silhouettes to the west, black flat land to the east.

**The ride.**
* It starts on the ferry from Den Helder to 't Horntje: hourly, about 20 minutes, on the Texelstroom [6][7].
* The island has 140 km of bike paths through dunes, woods and fields [8].
* The 65 km round the island route runs over the dike and past the harbour, the dunes and the lighthouse [9].
* Outside the villages the paths are unlit, because the rural street lamps were removed and never replaced [10].

**Skyline and landmarks.**
* **Eierland lighthouse, De Cocksdorp.** Bright red (a red coating since 1977), 34.7 m tall, standing on a 20 m dune, with its light 53 m above NAP [3][4][5][1].
  * It gives two white flashes every 10 s at 2.85 million candela, with a range of 29 nautical miles [3][5].
  * It was rebuilt wider after war damage in 1945 [3].
  * The sea now reaches its foot [1].
* **Den Hoorn church.** A white tower from 1450, with a searchlight on its balustrade that serves as a beacon for ships [12].
* **Hoge Berg.** Site of the Georgian war cemetery, with 481 graves [13][14].
* **Polder mill Het Noorden (1878).** Still pumping [15].

**Buildings.**
* **Schapenboeten (sheep sheds)** [16]:
  * small sheds for storing hay for the sheep, with a saddle roof cut off on one side
  * the flat end and the door face northeast, away from the southwest wind
  * roofs of reed or tiles, low walls, tiny windows
* **Tuunwallen (turf walls).** Stacked sods about 1 m high and 1 m wide, paler and yellower than the grass around them [17].

**Street furniture and light.**
* All street lamps were switched to LED in 2015, and the lights in village centres go off at 1 a.m. [10].
* Outside the villages there are hardly any lamp posts [11].

**Sky.**
* A measuring station at the south end reads 20.7 to 21.0 mag/arcsec² [20]. That is Bortle 4 to 5 [21]. Darker spots on the island are likely but have not been measured.
* In summer the Milky Way stands high overhead [20].
* There is no dark sky park on Texel. The Dutch ones are Boschplaat on Terschelling and Lauwersmeer [22]. Texel is, however, one of the frontrunners in the Wadden Sea effort to protect darkness [23].
* **Aurora over Texel:**
  * the night of 10 to 11 May 2024 [24]
  * the night of 10 to 11 October 2024 [25]
  * 20 January 2026, green and purple [27]
* **Nationwide:** the night of 11 to 12 November 2025, green and red [26].
* According to KNMI (the Dutch weather institute), aurora is only visible from the Netherlands near the peak of the 11 year solar cycle, and it shows mostly as a red glow [28].

**People.**
* 13,946 islanders [1].
* Sheep numbers have fallen from about 14,000 to 9,000 or 10,000. The island's 45 sheep farmers aim for 15,000 by 2035 [18][19].
* The local dialect, Tessels, says "huus" for house, "diek" for dike and "skéép" for sheep [1].
* Traditional island dress disappeared about a century ago [1].

**Sounds.**
* Herring gulls in large numbers, and spoonbills at De Muy [1].
* Lambs in spring, often born at night [19].
* The southwest wind [16].
* I found no source for the ferry horn.

**Night moments and folklore.**
* **Meierblis, 30 April.** About 110 bonfires were counted in 2003. They are said to chase off the spirits of winter [1].
* **Ouwe Sunderklaas, 12 December.** Islanders in masks, using disguised voices, go from door to door [30].
* **Sint Maarten, 11 November.** Children walk with lanterns [1].
* **The Sommeltjes of De Waal.** Small folk who polish the islanders' silver at night and vanish back into stone at sunrise [1].
* **Zeevonk in summer.** Blue glowing algae in the sea, best seen after midnight [29].

**Humour hooks.**
* Green "VRIJ" (free) parking signs near Den Burg that cannot be switched off, glowing in the dark [11].
* Texelaars born in Den Helder, because the island has no maternity ward, are called "Helderse kraaien" (Den Helder crows) [1].
* Island nicknames: Vogeleiland (bird island) and Het Gouden Bolletje (the golden bun) [1].

## 3. Groningen

**Essence.** The Martinitoren rising over a river of bicycles, and everyone saying "Moi" [7][1][18].

**Ground and topology.**
* Flat. The city sits on the last tip of the Hondsrug, a low sandy ridge [1][3].
* The centre is about 7 m above NAP [1]. The ridge's final bump, about 9 m, is visible in the Herestraat [3].
* A ring of canals, the Diepenring, wraps around the old town [4].
* At night: canals like black mirrors under a glowing lid of cloud [25].

**The ride.**
* Since 1977 the centre has been split into four sectors. Cars must detour round the Diepenring; bikes go straight through [4].
* The Diepenring road now has one car lane plus an extra wide bike path [4].
* Dutch bike paths are standard red asphalt [5].
* 57% of residents commute by bike [1], and more than 5,000 bikes park under the station square [2].
* From the station you ride across the museum island into town [9][10].

**Skyline and landmarks.**
* **Martinitoren**, nicknamed d'Olle Grieze (the old grey one) [6][7]:
  * 96.8 m tall, built of Bentheimer sandstone
  * galleries at 40, 55 and 68 m, topped by an open crown and a horse shaped weathervane from 1822
  * it leans about 0.6 m
  * new lighting since October 2025: warm at the foot, cooler at the top [6][8]
* **Groninger Museum (1994).** Built on islands in the canal: a yellow tower, a silver cylinder, a pale blue block in a deliberately broken, angular style (deconstructivist), and a brick base [9][10]. I found no source on how it is lit at night.
* **Forum (2019).** A 45 m tall building made of angled flat faces, with a roof terrace [11].
* **Peerd van Ome Loeks (1959).** A statue of a man and his horse in front of the station [24].

**Buildings.**
* The medieval Akerk, with merchant houses, warehouses and moored historic ships along the canal called the A [13][14].
* The east side of the Grote Markt was destroyed in the Second World War and rebuilt [12].
* The station dates from 1896 [2], and the Academiegebouw (the university's main building, 1909) is in neo Renaissance style [15].

**Street furniture and light.** The city's lighting plan uses warm white light of at least 3000 K [8]:
* hanging lamps, plus a soft wash of light on facades, in streets such as the Herestraat and Peperstraat
* tall lamp masts along the Diepenring, and low fittings on the quays
* amber accents on bridges at the entrances to the city
* string lights in the trees on the squares

**Sky.**
* Bright. On clear nights the sky reads 18.8 mag/arcsec² at De Held and 19.7 at the Zernike campus [25]. That is Bortle 6 to 7 [26].
* Cloudy nights are brighter still, around 17, because the clouds reflect the city's light back down [25].
* Real darkness starts 25 to 30 km to the north. Lauwersmeer Dark Sky Park (since 2016) peaks near 21.1, and Hornhuizen near 21.4 [25][27].
* Aurora reached the province on 20 January 2026 [28].

**People.**
* 244,807 inhabitants, about a quarter of them students [1].
* Locals call themselves Stadjers and call their city "Stad" or "Groot Loug" (big village) [2].
* "Moi" began as "good morning" and now means hello and even goodbye [18].
* Bars around the Grote Markt stay open until about 5 a.m. [1].

**Sounds.**
* **Martinitoren carillon** [16][17][7]:
  * 52 bells according to the local carillon foundation; other sources say 49 or 62
  * tunes played automatically by a pinned drum
  * played live on Tuesday from 12 to 13 and Saturday from 11 to 12
* The Academiegebouw carillon plays every half hour [17].
* One of the bells still has a bullet hole from 1945 [6].

**Night moments and folklore.**
* **28 August, Gronings Ontzet.** A fair and big fireworks to celebrate the failed 1672 siege by the bishop nicknamed "Bommen Berend" [21].
* **11 November, Sint Maarten.** Children with lanterns, in the city of Saint Martin [23][1].
* **11 to 13 December 2026, WinterWelVaart.** Lit historic ships along the A [22].
* **Ghost hook.** In 1577, celebration fires on the tower's third gallery brought the top down, and according to a chronicler 17 bells fell. A myth says the tower once stood 127 m tall [7][6].

**Humour hooks.**
* The city slogan "Er gaat niets boven Groningen" (Nothing beats Groningen, 1989) was launched with an ad showing aliens landing and being welcomed [19][20].
* "Peerd van Ome Loeks is dood" (Uncle Loeks's horse is dead), a cheerful song about a dead horse, usually linked to 1910 [24].
* The cliché of the stiff, unfriendly Groninger, answered by the slogan's own copywriter: "introvert maybe, not stiff" [20].

## Gaps and uncertainties

* **Urk**
  * I found no source for street lamp types or light colour.
  * There is no sky measurement in Urk itself; Weerribben is a proxy, and the 27 km distance is my own calculation from coordinates.
  * I found no reliable source for a typical Urk greeting.
  * Night lighting on the wind turbines is not verified.
  * The Commons photo "Licht in de duisternis" has a 12:39 timestamp, so check that it really is a night shot.
  * The church bell is dated 1456 in the church article but 1461 in the monument list.
* **Texel**
  * The only sky measuring station is at 53.003 N, 4.787 E. I take this to be the sea research institute (NIOZ) at 't Horntje near the ferry; that is my inference. The dunes and the north end are probably darker than the readings.
  * An AD article with figures on unlit bike paths was behind a privacy gate, so I did not use it.
  * I found no source for the ferry horn or for the colour temperature of the street lamps.
  * The November 2025 aurora report is national (from Nijmegen), not specific to Texel.
* **Groningen**
  * The number of carillon bells differs by source: 52, 49 or 62.
  * I found nothing on how the Groninger Museum is lit at night.
  * The traffic plan is dated 1977 in its own article and 1976 in the city article.
  * Brick paving in the streets is not sourced.
* **All three places:** the Bortle classes are my own conversions from the sky brightness readings, using the approximate table on Wikipedia. They are not official ratings.

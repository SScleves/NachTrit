## 1. Zeeland

**Essence.** You ride along the sea defences, not through towns. Almost all of Zeeland except the dunes lies at or below sea level, a patchwork of polders and dikes [Z7]. The provincial coat of arms shows a lion struggling out of the waves, with the motto Luctor et Emergo [Z7]. The icon is the Oosterscheldekering. Queen Beatrix closed it on 4 October 1986, saying "Zeeland is veilig" [Z1].

**Ground and topology.**
* The polders lie at about sea level [Z7]. The only height is the dunes, which reach 52 m above NAP at Groot Valkenisse [Z8].
* The barrier closes when +3.00 m NAP is forecast [Z1].
* In 1953 the sea reached +4.15 m NAP on the west side of the Oosterschelde and +4.35 m NAP at Westkapelle [Z1, Z9].
* The tidal range at Yerseke is 3.25 m [Z6].
* At night, picture black water on one side of the road and lower black fields on the other (inference).

**The ride.**
* **Oosterscheldekering (N57).**
  * A 9 km road link between Schouwen‑Duiveland and Noord‑Beveland [Z1], with two lanes and an 80 km/h limit [Z5]. Cyclists may ride it [Z6].
  * About 4 km of it can close. It has 65 hollow concrete pillars, 30 to 40 m tall and up to 18,000 t each. Between them hang 62 steel gates, about 42 m wide and 6 to 12 m high [Z1, Z2].
  * Hydraulic cylinders rise above every pillar beside the road. How far they stick up shows how deep the water is at that spot [Z1].
  * Three gate sections alternate with two artificial islands [Z2].
  * Halfway is the island Neeltje Jans. It has the Topshuis control room, a radar tower, and a spare 66th pillar that has stood alone in its dock since about 1980 [Z1, Z21].
  * A full closure takes about 75 minutes [Z1].
  * **Lighting: unverified.** OpenStreetMap has no lighting tag on the barrier road and marks the cycle paths on Neeltje Jans as unlit [Z5]. My art suggestion: a dark road, with Neeltje Jans as the one lit island.
* **Zeelandbrug (N256).**
  * 5,022 m long: 54 piers, 52 identical 95 m spans and one 40 m opening span. Opened 15 December 1965 [Z3].
  * Two lanes, a narrow cycle path, no hard shoulder, concrete barriers on both sides and a double solid centre line [Z3].
  * OpenStreetMap marks the fixed spans as unlit and the opening span, near the Schouwen end, as lit [Z4].
* **Westkappelse Zeedijk.** 5 km of sea dike carrying the N287. The land side is green, the sea side is asphalt [Z9].

**Skyline and landmarks.**
* **Lange Jan, Middelburg:** abbey tower with eight sides, 90.4 m, faced in pale limestone (ledesteen) [Z11].
* **Dikke Toren, Zierikzee:** 62 m, never finished, with a ship‑shaped weathervane [Z12].
* **Grote Kerk, Veere:** a heavy west tower that stopped after one stage [Z13].
* **Westkapelle Hoog:** a 52.3 m brick church tower turned lighthouse, with a red lantern on top. It gives one flash every 3 seconds, visible 28 nautical miles away [Z10].
* **Plompe Toren:** a lone 23 m tower set into the dike, the last trace of the drowned village of Koudekerke. The legend says a mermaid's curse would leave only the tower standing [Z14, Z15].

**Buildings.** Big barns of black tarred wood with sharp white frames, painted so the door can be found in the dark [Z16].

**Street furniture and light.** Rural lamp posts are being removed. The municipality of Veere took out 118 outside built up areas in 2022 [Z23].

**Sky.** Estimated Bortle class 4 (sky quality about 21) at the barrier, the bridge and Westkapelle [Z26]. That is the "rural to suburban transition" class [Z27]. The lighthouse beam sweeps through the fog [Z10].

**People.**
* **Walcheren traditional dress, women:** black merino jacket, lace cap, a gold forehead band with gold spirals at the temples, Sunday pendants with a pearl, and a blood coral necklace with a gold clasp. In mourning, everything is black [Z17].
* **Men:** black felt, two gold buttons at the neck, a round hat [Z17].
* Only 157 women still wore the dress daily in 2003; the youngest was 69 [Z17].
* Mussel fishermen of Yerseke, home of the only mussel auction in the Netherlands [Z20].
* **Speech:** no h ("uus" for huis), "diek" for dijk, "muus" for muis. "Naer‑uus" means going home [Z18].

**Sounds.** Wind and sea. The rhythm of tyres over the Zeelandbrug, which locals name as a sound of Zeeland [Z24]. Lange Jan's carillon every quarter hour [Z11].

**Night moments.**
* The flood night of 31 January to 1 February 1953: dikes broke from 03:00, and 1,836 people died in the Netherlands [Z19].
* Storm closures of the barrier [Z1].
* NK Tegenwindfietsen, the national headwind cycling championship over the barrier, held only at wind force 7 or more [Z22].
* Nacht van de Nacht, a national night‑sky event in late October [Z28].

**Humour hooks.**
* The headwind championship is ridden on gearless bikes with coaster brakes. It is announced with the mock Zeeuws "We kriehen sturm" [Z22].
* Zeeuwen used to overcorrect the missing h into a g when speaking standard Dutch [Z18].
* Thrift: the bridge toll was meant to end around 1978 but lasted until 1993 [Z3]. The Westerschelde tunnel had toll‑free Saturdays and only became free for cars on 30 December 2024 [Z25].
* Two great church towers were left unfinished because the money ran out [Z12, Z13].
* The spare pillar is still waiting for a job [Z21].

## 2. Den Haag and Scheveningen

**Essence.** Parliament and government sit here, although Amsterdam has been the capital since 1808 [H1]. The picture: the Gothic Ridderzaal reflected in the Hofvijver, then one straight road to the sea, the Pier and the Kurhaus.

**Ground and topology.**
* The city stands on old dunes and peat. The old story puts the posh Hagenaar on sand and the plain Hagenees on peat, divided roughly by the Laan van Meerdervoort [H1, H16].
* There are 11 km of coast and almost 12 km of beach, backed by dunes [H1].
* In the Westduinpark the dunes rise above 30 m. The highest, De Vulkaan, is 33 m [H11].
* The Hofvijver started as a natural dune lake [H4].
* The Scheveningen boulevard hides the sea dike, which was strengthened from 2009 to 2013 [H9].

**The ride.**
* **Scheveningseweg:** a dead straight road from the old centre to Scheveningen, built in 1665 to a design by Constantijn Huygens. The first Dutch tram, pulled by horses, ran along it from 1864. Grass has grown between its tram rails since 1973 [H10].
* **The boulevard,** designed by De Solà Morales [H9]:
  * Walkers, cyclists and cars each have their own level.
  * Two light blue footbridges cross over the cycle path.
  * Five 15 m blocks of rusted Corten steel stand along it.
  * Surfaces are natural stone, clinker bricks, and asphalt mixed with shells.
* **Cycle paths** are red, as everywhere in the Netherlands [H27].
* **Trams:**
  * The older GTL trams are red with beige bands and are being replaced from 2026 to 2028 [H24].
  * The new TINA trams are red and off white, carrying passengers since 7 September 2026 [H23].

**Skyline and landmarks.**
* **Ridderzaal:** a medieval hall with a 25.74 m ridge, where the King normally gives the annual speech from the throne [H3]. The Binnenhof has been under renovation since 2021 and should be back in use in mid 2031. A temporary lookout tower has stood on the Buitenhof since 2024 [H2].
* **Hofvijver:** a pond with a small island carrying a flagpole, and a fountain. In 2024 a floating block of builders' huts lay in the pond [H4].
* **The Pier (1961):** a covered promenade with an open deck on top, three islands and a lookout platform at 45 m. It stands on 187 concrete pillars and originally reached 381 m into the sea [H5]. A 50 m Ferris wheel has stood on it since 2016 [H6].
* **Kurhaus:** a grand hotel built 1884 to 1887 [H8].
* **Lighthouse:** red cast iron with twelve sides, 30 m tall. The light sits at 49 m and gives two flashes every 10 seconds, with a range of 29 nautical miles [H7].
* **Government towers near Den Haag Centraal** [H12, H13]:
  * the twin JuBi ministry towers, 146 m
  * Hoftoren, 142 m
  * City Tower, 141.8 m
  * Strijkijzer, 132 m, easy to spot by its height, colour and top
  * De Kroon, 131.5 m

**Buildings and details.** Tom Otterness's 23 bronze fairytale figures sit on the boulevard steps by the Beelden aan Zee museum. The obelisk De Naald dates from 1865 [H9].

**Street furniture and light.** The city switched from 2024 to smart LED street lights that are warmer and more directed [H22]. Overall the city is bright at night (see Sky).

**Sky.**
* Estimated Bortle class 9 in the centre and 8 in Scheveningen [H21], the "inner city" and "city" classes [H28].
* To the south glows the orange light of the Westland greenhouses, nicknamed the Glass City [H20].
* In most of the Netherlands only a few dozen bright stars are visible [H29].
* The lighthouse flashes over the surf [H7].

**People.**
* **Civil servants:** the Hoftoren alone houses the Ministry of Education, Culture and Science and agencies such as DUO [H14].
* **Two kinds of locals:** the posh Hagenaar speaks "hoog" Haags, the plain Hagenees "plat" Haags [H16].
* **Scheveningen traditional dress for women** [H15]:
  * a long black skirt
  * an apron, blue striped on weekdays and glossy black on Sundays
  * a pastel shawl folded into a triangle
  * a white cap over a silver head band whose ornaments show at the front
  * on Sundays, a necklace of red jet, garnet or blood coral with a gold clasp
* Only a few women still wear it: 460 in 1986, 85 in 2000, the youngest born in 1933. Locals find the nicknames "Kniertje" and "visvrouw" (fishwife) offensive, so the game should avoid them [H15].
* **Words:** "dûnah" means the dunes and "groen pikkie" a gherkin [H16]. In street slang the city is called Agga [H1].

**Sounds.** Expect surf, gulls and tram wheels (inference). The crown of the Strijkijzer tower used to whistle in strong wind [H13].

**Night moments.**
* **New Year bonfires** burn on the beaches of Scheveningen and Duindorp. In 2018/19 they reached 38.7 m and showered the village with sparks. They are now capped at 10 by 10 by 10 m and are lit a day early when the wind is too strong [H18].
* **New Year countdown:** since 2017 the national countdown and its fireworks happen on the Hofvijver [H4].
* **Prinsjesdag** falls on the third Tuesday of September. It is a daytime ceremony [H25].
* **Summer fireworks festival:** it was fired from a pontoon 265 m offshore, but has not been held since 2019 [H19].

**Humour hooks.**
* **Haagse bluf:** a dessert of whipped egg white and redcurrant juice. It is named after the airs Hagenaars supposedly put on: it looks like a lot but is mostly air [H26].
* **Tower nicknames** [H12]:
  * De Vulpen (fountain pen), the Hoftoren
  * De Afstandsbediening (remote control)
  * De Citruspers (lemon squeezer)
  * De Keukenrol (kitchen roll)
* **Hagenaar versus Hagenees,** posh against plain [H16].
* **"Scheveningen" as a password:** an anecdote says it was used in the war to unmask German spies, who pronounced the "Sch" wrong [H17]. For NachTrit, the dead who forgot Dutch cannot say it.
* **Little Jantje:** a statue on the bank of the Hofvijver points at the Binnenhof, where his father lives in the nursery rhyme [H4].

## 3. Rotterdam

**Essence.** People call it "Manhattan aan de Maas" [R27]. It is a city of towers rebuilt after the 1940 bombing, with the pale blue Erasmusbrug, nicknamed De Zwaan (the swan), at its heart [R1].

**Ground and topology.**
* More than three quarters of the city lies below sea level [R27].
* The lowest point in the Netherlands, 6.76 m below NAP, is next door in Nieuwerkerk aan den IJssel [R28].
* The Maastunnel goes down to about 20 m below NAP [R26].
* At night the Nieuwe Maas is a wide black band between two lit shores (inference).

**The ride.**
* **Erasmusbrug:** 802 m long, opened 4 September 1996 [R1, R2].
  * A 284 m cable stayed span hangs from a 139 m bent, lopsided steel pylon. An 89 m lifting section lies between the pylon and the Kop van Zuid [R1].
  * The deck carries four traffic lanes with two tram tracks in the middle. Cycle and foot paths hang 6.7 m out on both sides. Ships get 12.5 m clearance over a 200 m stretch [R2].
  * The bridge runs from the centre in the north west to the Kop van Zuid in the south east. Heading south it points about 132 degrees; I calculated this from OpenStreetMap [R1, R5].
* **Maastunnel (1942):** it has a separate tube for cyclists, reached by wooden escalators and lined with yellow tiles. About 7,000 cyclists use it every day [R26].
* **Willemsbrug (1981):** the deck hangs from two red yokes, 50 m tall [R22].
* **Cycle lanes and trams:** cycle lanes are red [R41]. RET trams have been silver grey with blue and red stripes since 2020 [R33].

**Riding south east (the classic view).** The white pylon grows ahead, its cables fanning down toward you. Behind it, from left to right:
1. The red yokes of the Willemsbrug, far left.
2. The Maastoren, ahead left.
3. The green pixel front of De Link, straight ahead.
4. The stacked bulk of De Rotterdam, just right.
5. New Orleans, the World Port Center, Hotel New York, and Montevideo with its M on the tip of the pier.

The Euromast stands alone far to the right like a needle. De Zalmhaven stays behind your right shoulder.

**Riding north west into the centre.**
* De Zalmhaven, the tallest thing in view, rises 0.7 km ahead and 0.2 km left. The Euromast is further left, 1.3 km out.
* The Cooltoren is 1 km ahead, slightly right.
* The cluster of towers on the Weena (Millenniumtoren, FIRST, Delftse Poort) is 2.1 km ahead and 0.2 to 0.5 km right.
* The Witte Huis, the cube houses and the Markthal lie 0.75 to 1.1 km ahead and 0.9 to 1.1 km right.
* Several towers are still under construction in 2026 [R6], so add a tower crane or two (inference).

**Buildings.**
* **Witte Huis (1897):** modelled on American skyscrapers, it was once the tallest office building in Europe and survived the 1940 bombing [R23].
* **Cube houses:** 38 yellow homes on six sided columns at Blaak, each with a three sided pyramid on top. Piet Blom designed them; they were built 1982 to 1984. Trams and the metro run underneath [R24].
* **Markthal (2014):** a horseshoe arch of grey natural stone with glass ends. Inside, an 11,000 m² mural shows giant fruit, fish and flowers [R25].

**Street furniture and light.**
* **Street lights:** about 100,000 light poles were switched to LED between 2020 and 2025 [R32]. That suggests mostly white light rather than orange (inference).
* **Erasmusbrug lighting:**
  * The lit cables make the bridge visible from far away. There are lamps over the lanes and light under the deck.
  * The LEDs are partly white and partly coloured, and are dimmed or partly switched off late at night [R3].
  * Colour schemes switch on a quarter of an hour before sunset [R4], on at most 30 occasions a year [R1].
* **Brandgrens:** since 14 May 2010 a 12 km line of lamps in the pavement marks where the 1940 fire stopped. They show red flames, and green ones on the Noordereiland [R29].

**Sky.** Estimated Bortle class 9 at the Kop van Zuid [R31], the "inner city" class [R43]. Shell Pernis, the largest refinery in Europe, stands on the Vondelingenplaat and has burned off gas in flares during shutdowns [R30]. Port cranes stay lit at night [R44].

**People.**
* Feyenoord plays in red and white; its anthem is "Hand in Hand" and its stadium De Kuip [R39].
* "City of the port and the workers" is one of Rotterdam's own clichés [R35].
* **Speech:**
  * "ei" becomes "aai"
  * "hebbie" for "heb je"
  * the final r drops
  * "kennen" and "kunnen" get swapped [R34]

**Sounds.**
* Trams crossing the bridge; the tram tracks are confirmed [R2].
* The stay cables once shook in wind and rain and needed dampers, and the deck trembled under a dance parade [R1].
* Ship horns on the Maas and the clatter of the tunnel's wooden escalators (inference).

**Night moments.**
* **Bridge colours:** orange on 26 and 27 April (King's Night and King's Day), red, white and blue on 5 May [R4], and rainbow colours during Rotterdam Pride in the last week of September [R1].
* **Wereldhavendagen (World Port Days):** the first weekend of September, on the river around the bridge [R40, R1].
* **14 May:** the anniversary of the 1940 bombing, along the Brandgrens lights [R29].

**Humour hooks.**
* **"Niet lullen maar poetsen"** (stop talking, start scrubbing). Nobody knows who said it first [R36].
* **The city motto "Sterker door strijd"** (stronger through struggle) [R27].
* **The three city saying:** "In Rotterdam they earn it, in The Hague they divide it, in Amsterdam they blow it." A quote site credits the Rotterdam poet Jules Deelder, but the attribution is uncertain [R37]. It ties all three chapters together.
* **The kapsalon:** fries, shawarma, melted cheese and salad, invented in 2003 by a Rotterdam barber and named after his salon [R38].

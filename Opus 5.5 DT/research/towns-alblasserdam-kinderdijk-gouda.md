# NachTrit art direction research: Alblasserdam, Kinderdijk and Dordrecht, Gouda

All three places can be told apart at a glance. Alblasserdam is a dark dike village beside a busy river, with shipyards and a medieval tower. Kinderdijk is rows of mills across black water, and Dordrecht has a stubby, leaning church tower above three rivers. Gouda is a warm, candlelit market square around a toy‑like town hall. Each citation code points to the source list under that place. The Bortle values are model estimates from Clear Outside, not measurements taken on site. Anything I could not confirm is marked uncertain. Today is 27 October 2026, so some 2026 events have already passed.

## 1. Alblasserdam (the hero's home town)

**Essence.** A dike village on the tidal Noord, where a superyacht shipyard sits next to a 15th century church tower. It is also where the bike ride to the Kinderdijk mills starts [A1][A2][A3].

**Ground.** The land is flat peat meadow. The western Alblasserwaard lies about 1.5 m below sea level, with clay on peat, crossed by ditches and enclosed by a ring dike organised in 1277 [A4]. The town takes its name from a dam in the small river Alblas. That dam became a sluice in 1599 and was turned back into a dam in 1997 [A1]. The Noord is tidal, so its current changes direction with the tide [A21]. I could not find the height of the dike crest (uncertain). Art note: on the polder side the land falls away into blackness, while the river side is full of moving ship lights.

**The ride.** The dike roads Oost Kinderdijk and West Kinderdijk are lined with dike houses [A5]. The Brug over de Noord (1939) is an arch bridge with a bascule span; in 1939 it was billed as Europe's largest bascule bridge [A6][A7]. Since the A15 moved into the Noordtunnel in 1992, the bridge carries only local traffic and dangerous goods [A6]. A tide gauge is mounted on it [A24]. Waterbus catamarans pass on the river, and most of their passengers are cyclists [A8].

**Skyline.**
* Oude Toren: a plain 15th century brick tower with pointed arch niches, a frieze of small round arches, a short octagonal spire and a Hemony bell from 1659 [A9].
* Grote Kerk on Cortgene: neo Gothic, built 1899 [A5].
* Oceanco shipyard, which builds yachts of 80 m and more. Koru, a 127 m three master with a navy blue hull, was built here [A2][A3][A10].
* On the riverbank: the BCTN container terminal and the former Nedstaal steelworks, which ran from 1938 to 2017 [A11][A12].
* De Blokker: a small wipmolen from 1630. It burned in 1997 and has been working again since 2001 [A13].

I found nothing on how any of these are lit at night (uncertain).

**Buildings.** Single storey dike houses from the mid 19th century, with high hipped roofs, shutters and six pane sash windows. There is also a 17th century thatched farmhouse on the dike, and the Huis van Zessen (1923), a modern dike house by Cornelis van Eesteren with colours by Theo van Doesburg [A5][A14]. The 1940 bombing destroyed the centre but spared Kerkstraat and the dike houses [A1].

**Light.** About 60 percent of street lights were LED by March 2024, and the rest were due within two years. The lights can be dimmed to protect hedgehogs. So the town has white LED light and almost no orange sodium; the colour temperature is not published [A15].

**Sky.** The town is Bortle 7 (SQM about 18.7); inland at De Donk it is Bortle 6 [A16][A17]. At those levels the Milky Way is invisible and light domes sit on the horizon [A18]. Rotterdam glows to the northwest and Dordrecht to the southwest [A4].

**People.** This is a Bible Belt town: 15 churches for 20,239 people [A1][A4]. In the orthodox Reformed congregations, Sunday means two or three services, women in hats and psalm singing [A19]. The local dialect is fading. Examples: veul (veel), hai zee (hij zei), and laeg, which means both low and empty [A20].

**Sounds.** Ship traffic on one of Western Europe's busiest waterways [A2]; the 1659 bell; psalms; in winter, up to 3,700 wigeon roosting on the Kinderdijk boezems next door [A22].

**Night moments.** The Havenfestival week in mid September (in 2026, roads closed on 15 and 19 September) [A23]. The Kinderdijk Verlichtingsweek (see section 2). When the boezems freeze, the Molentocht skating tour, sometimes more than 10,000 skaters a day [A1].

**Humour hooks.**
* A yacht from this village nearly got Rotterdam's De Hef bridge taken apart. In the end it left without its masts [A1][A10].
* The smallest of the 19 famous mills belongs to Alblasserdam, not Kinderdijk [A13].
* The dam became a sluice, and then a dam again [A1].
* A Mondrian was auctioned to pay for restoring the local De Stijl house [A14].

## 2. Kinderdijk and Dordrecht

**Essence.**
* Kinderdijk: 19 mills in rows along long water, on the UNESCO list since 1997. It is the largest surviving group of old mills in the world [K1][K2].
* Dordrecht ("Dordt"): the oldest city of Holland, with a stubby, leaning tower above a junction of three rivers [K3][K4].

**Ground.**
* The polder lies about 1.5 m below sea level. The mills lift water about 1.40 m per step, in two steps, into high reservoirs (boezems), which are emptied into the Lek at low tide [K2][K5].
* Kinderdijk sits where the Noord meets the Lek [K1].
* Dordrecht covers an island where the Merwede splits into the Noord and the Oude Maas. Water there lies about 1.5 m below NAP, and the highest natural point is +2.60 m NAP [K3].
* The Voorstraat is itself a dike; with stop logs in place it holds water up to +3.60 m NAP. Cellars in the old harbour area still flood [K6][K7].

**The ride.**
* Kinderdijk: narrow embankments between reeds and water. The mill rows are seen on foot or by bike, and the paths are shared and mostly dark [K2][K8]. The Driehoeksveer ferry (Ridderkerk, Kinderdijk, Krimpen aan de Lek) makes its last crossings around 17:30 to 18:00. During the Verlichtingsweek the WaterShuttle and the shuttle bus also leave before 18:00, so at night you arrive by bike [K8][K9].
* Dordrecht: the quays of Wolwevershaven and Kuipershaven are joined by the Damiatebrug, an iron double drawbridge from 1855 [K10][K11]. Then come the 1.2 km Voorstraat, partly pedestrian [K6], and the Groothoofd quay where the rivers meet [K12]. The Waterbus links Papendrecht with the Merwekade, which makes a good chapter transition [K30].

**Skyline.**
* Nederwaard: 8 round brick mills from 1738, set in a staggered line. They are ground sailers, so the sails (27.5 to 28 m across) sweep low over the yards [K2][K13][K31].
* Overwaard: 8 octagonal wooden mills from 1740, thatched and standing in one straight line; sails 28.6 to 29.5 m [K2][K13].
* J.U. Smit pumping station (1972), with Archimedes screws you can see from outside, and the 1868 Wisboom station, now a visitor centre [K1][K13].
* Grote Kerk tower: 65 m of Ettringer tuff, 2.25 m out of plumb. It was meant to reach 108 m but was capped in 1626 with four clock faces. Scaffolding with a catch screen is going up until the end of 2026 [K4][K14][K15].
* Groothoofdspoort: a city gate on the quay, with a dome from 1692 [K12].
* Kyck over den Dyck: a brick stage mill from 1713, the last mill left in the city [K16].

**Buildings.** The mills are family homes; the Nederwaard mills are round inside [K13][K26]. Dordrecht has more than 900 rijksmonumenten and merchant houses along its harbours [K3]. It also has about 50 Dordtse gevels: stepped gables with round arches and trefoils, two windows per floor [K17]. The town hall has a neoclassical front (1834 to 1841) with cast iron lions [K18].

**Light.**
* Kinderdijk: the mill field is lit only one week a year, and visitors are told to bring a torch [K8][K26]. A 2009 photo shows a mill lit green, so the colour may change from year to year (uncertain) [K34].
* Dordrecht: monuments are floodlit, but at times the lights have been switched off (I only saw a headline, uncertain) [K19]. In December the Dwaalspoor route adds light art [K20]. I could not find which street lamp model the city uses (uncertain).

**Sky.** Bortle 7 in both places: SQM about 18.6 at Kinderdijk and 18.75 in Dordrecht (model estimates) [K21][K22]. Rotterdam glows to the northwest [K32].

**People.** Millers read the wind and the clouds and take a calling cuckoo as a sign of rain. In the past they had to keep pumping through rainy nights. Today they keep vegetable gardens and animals [K23]. People from Dordrecht walk "op" the Voorstraat, never "in", because it is a dike [K6]. Dordts dialect uses diminutives ending in ie (bruggie, kinnechie), ends sentences with "hé" and stresses the word Grotekérk on the last syllable [K24].

**Sounds.**
* The Grote Kerk carillon has 67 bells and a 9,830 kg bourdon. Since 2020 the church also has the first English style change ringing peal in the Netherlands [K4][K14].
* Ship traffic at what Wikipedia calls Europe's busiest water junction [K3].
* In the reeds: purple heron, black tern and spotted crake; wigeon in winter [K25][K33].

**Night moments.**
* Verlichtingsweek, always the first full week of September. In 2026 it ran 7 to 12 September, lit from about 20:15 to 22:30, and until 23:00 on Friday and Saturday. In 2027 it should fall on 6 to 11 September (my inference from the rule, not announced) [K8][K26].
* Dordtse Decemberdagen: the Dwaalspoor light walk, about 1.5 km, open 17:00 to 22:00. The 2025/26 edition ran 10 December to 3 January; 2026/27 dates are not published yet (uncertain). There is also a Wintertuin by the Grote Kerk between Christmas and New Year, an ice rink, and boat tours past lit harbours [K20][K27].
* Dordt in Stoom, a steam festival, next runs 19 to 21 May 2028 [K28].

**Humour hooks.**
* The legend of the name: after the 1421 flood a cradle drifted to the dike. A cat jumped from side to side to keep it level, and the baby inside was asleep and dry [K1].
* Mill language: setting the lower sail just past the middle of the mill body, on the right, means mourning. For a death in the miller's family it stayed that way for a year and six weeks [K29]. It suits a land of the dead.
* The round mills make choosing furniture "an extra challenge" [K13].
* Dordrecht's oldest church is called the New Church (Nieuwkerk) [K4].
* The Damiatebrug opened in April 1855 and collapsed in May [K11].
* Kinderdijk had the first electricity supply in the Netherlands (1886), yet its mill field stays dark [K1][K26].

## 3. Gouda

**Essence.** A Gothic town hall with red and white shutters, standing on its own in the middle of the fan shaped Markt. And cheese [G2][G3][G6].

**Ground.** Gouda is built on peat and is sinking. The centre lies about 0.5 m below sea level, the outer districts about 2 m below, and the new district Westergouwe about 6 m below. The centre sinks about 2 mm a year, the outskirts up to 2 cm [G4]. Just to the west, the Zuidplaspolder holds the lowest point in the Netherlands, 6.78 m below NAP [G5]. The rivers are the Hollandse IJssel and the Gouwe. The Schielands Hoge Zeedijk, a 13th century dike that protects 3 million people, ends at the Gouwe [G6][G7].

**The ride.** Along the IJssel past the 1883 water tower (33 m) [G8]. Through the Mallegatsluis lock into the Turfsingel canal, then along Westhaven and Oosthaven to the Tolhuis and its lock gates. At Achter de Kerk a narrow canal disappears under the houses [G6]. Wikipedia says the longest lock in Europe lies on this route through the centre (uncertain) [G6]. Since 2018 the old centre has hanging "tulip" lanterns: HTA LED, 2750 lumen, 3000 K warm white [G9].

**Skyline.**
* Town hall (Stadhuis): built 1448 to 1450, with its tower finished in 1459. It is made of Belgian limestone (Gobertange), has a Renaissance front stoop from 1603 and statues added in 1960 and 1961. It is under renovation through 2026 [G2][G3][G10].
* Sint-Janskerk: at 123 m the longest church in the Netherlands. It has wooden vaults because stone ones were too heavy for the peat. The tower is about 62 to 65 m (uncertain) [G11][G12].
* Gouwekerk: red brick with a partly open, six sided spire about 80 m tall, the highest point in town [G13].
* The Waag (weigh house, 1668, Pieter Post), with a white Carrara marble relief of cheese being weighed [G14].
* De Roode Leeuw: a round stone stage mill on the Vest, built around 1727 [G15].

**Buildings.** About 330 rijksmonumenten, canal houses, almshouse courtyards (Hofje van Cincq, Hofje van Letmaet), the late Gothic house De vier gekroonden and a stearine candle factory [G1][G6]. The municipal logo shows the six stars of the city arms and an open town hall shutter [G16].

**Light.** Warm white LED lanterns. On Kaarslicht night the street and shop lights are switched off. Candles burn in the town hall windows (about 1,500 of them) and in the upper floors around the Markt [G9][G17].

**Sky.** Bortle 8 (SQM 17.9, model estimate), the brightest sky of the three places [G18]. Gouda lies between Rotterdam and Utrecht [G1].

**People.**
* Locals are called Gouwenaars [G19].
* On market Thursdays, volunteer cheese farmers, traders and kaasmeisjes close deals by slapping hands (handjeklap) [G20][G21]. I could not verify what they wear (uncertain).
* A bronze statue, De Kaasboerin, of a farmer's wife clutching a cheese has stood on the Nieuwe Markt since 1988 [G6].
* Old Gouda dialect dropped the h: "en ontjie in en okkie", a little dog in a little hutch [G22].

**Sounds.** Every half hour the town hall clock show shows Count Floris V granting the city rights in 1272 [G2][G6]. The Sint-Jan carillon was cast by Hemony in 1676 and now has 50 bells [G11]. The town has two protected barrel organs, De Pansfluiter and De Lekkerkerker [G6].

**Night moments.**
* Gouda bij Kaarslicht, 71st edition, Friday 11 December 2026. The Norwegian tree from Kongsberg (20 to 30 m tall, 10,000 LEDs) is lit around 19:00. Because of the renovation, this year's ceremony is at the Waag, with the tree placed between the Waag and the town hall's old execution platform (schavot) [G10][G17][G23].
* Oranjenacht, the night before King's Day [G6].
* The cheese market (daytime) ran on Thursdays from 2 April to 27 August 2026, 10:00 to 12:30 [G24].

**Humour hooks.**
* The Dutch say Gouda with a throaty g, /ˈɣɑu̯.daː/. English speakers say GOW da or GOO da [G19].
* The town hall looks like a toy and has even been modelled in Madurodam [G3].
* The inn De Zalm had to stay lower than the Waag. Its gable stone reads "Niet te hooch niet te laech" (not too high, not too low) [G14].
* Stroopwafels were reportedly sold as cheap "armenkoeken" (poor people's cookies) made from crumbs and syrup [G25].
* An old Gouda put down that suits ghosts who forgot Dutch: "praat je moers taal en houd je stadhuiswoorden voor je", meaning speak your mother tongue and keep your town hall words to yourself [G22].
* The local subsidence walk will be "1 centimetre lower" next year [G4].

## What I could not confirm

* **Scaffolding decision.** The Dordrecht church tower and the Gouda town hall are both under scaffolding in 2026 [K15][G10]. You need to decide whether the game shows them clean or wrapped. The wrapped version could also work as a joke.
* **Sky darkness.** The Bortle figures are model estimates, not measurements taken on site.
* **Gouda glow.** Wikipedia's pages on the Zuidplaspolder and nearby villages say nothing about greenhouses, so I left out any greenhouse glow on Gouda's horizon.
* **Not found:**
  * the height of the dike crests (the AHN height viewer would give it)
  * the street lamp model and colour temperature in Dordrecht and Alblasserdam
  * when Dordrecht's monuments are floodlit
  * how Alblasserdam's landmarks are lit at night
  * what the Gouda cheese market volunteers wear
* **Mill lighting colour.** The only evidence for the colour of the Kinderdijk lighting is the 2009 photo with green light.
* **Dates not yet published.** The 2027 Verlichtingsweek dates are my inference from the "first full week of September" rule. The 2026/27 Dwaalspoor dates have not been announced.
* **Alblasserdam photos.** Wikimedia Commons has only the two night street photos of the town; the dike and the bridge appear only in daytime shots.

# NachTrit art direction: four Dutch towns at night

Each town has one signature look:

* **Utrecht:** a canal on two levels under a single tall tower.
* **Nijmegen:** a real hill above a wide river, plus a bridge whose lights march across it at sunset.
* **Maastricht:** a valley with a red church tower, an old stone bridge and carnival.
* **Amsterdam:** leaning canal houses and bridges outlined in small bulbs.

Elevations marked "AHN" are my own readings from the official Dutch elevation model (AHN4, bare ground, 5 m grid). For Nijmegen and Maastricht I read them along the street lines from OpenStreetMap. Treat them as approximate. "Art note" marks a design suggestion, not a sourced fact.

## 1. Utrecht

**Essence.** A lone medieval tower above a canal whose quays lie a level below the street, cellar restaurants glowing at the waterline, and bikes everywhere [U1][U2][U3].

**Ground and topology.** Flat. The city sits about 5 m above sea level [U3]; AHN gives Domplein 5.0 m and Neude 2.6 m above NAP [U4]. The Oudegracht runs about 2 km south to north through the centre [U2]. The wharves are about 2 m below the street [U5]. Art note: a lit trench cut through a dark, level town.

**The ride.** You ride the upper quay street with railings over the drop, or take a sloping ramp (a "wed", one survives at Ganzenmarkt) down to the wharf at water level [U2]. Brick bridges such as the Gaardbrug and Bakkerbrug cross the canal [U2]. 51% of trips in the city are by bike [U3], and Dutch bike paths are usually brick red [U6].

**Landmarks.**
* **Dom tower.** 112.32 m, the tallest church tower in the country [U1].
    * Three stages: a fortress-like square block (38.6 m), a lighter square (29.5 m) and an open eight-sided lantern (26 m) [U7].
    * Brick core clad in imported stone such as Bentheim sandstone. It was originally painted brick red with a bright white lantern [U7].
    * New lighting by Speirs Major has been on since 9 November 2024. Part of it shines from inside the tower. The main light goes off at 23:00 and the windows stay lit one hour longer [U8][U9].
* **Domplein.** An open square where a storm destroyed the church nave in 1674. The paving traces the lost floor plan [U1].
* **Trajectum Lumen.** Light art on the Oudegracht bridges and churches, from sunset to midnight. The source only confirms it until 2018, so its current state is uncertain [U10].

**Buildings.** Merchants' houses stand on raised banks. Their cellars are tunnelled under the street and open onto the wharf behind iron balustrades. Some houses stand with their back walls in the water [U2]. A local blog counts 732 wharf cellars (uncertain) [U11]. Many are restaurants with waterside terraces, partly supplied by beer boat [U2].

**Street furniture and light.** I could not verify the quay lamp models (uncertain). Dutch towns have been replacing orange sodium lamps with white LED since 2005 [U12]. Medium brightness: lit wharves over black water.

**Sky.** A glow from the surrounding cities. Most Dutch skies show only a few dozen stars [U13]. The land is so flat that from the tower top you can see Amsterdam and Rotterdam on clear days [U1].

**People.** Students (Utrecht University about 30,000, Hogeschool Utrecht 37,000) on upright steel bikes [U3]. Dialect: "Utreg" for Utrecht, and "jochie" as a friendly way to address someone [U14].

**Sounds.** Fourteen swinging bells (32 tonnes in total) rung by hand by a guild, plus a 50-bell carillon [U1].

**Night moments.**
* Sint Maarten Parade, Saturday 7 November 2026: a lantern route of about 4 km past the Domkerk. Lights go out at 21:00 [U15][U16].
* King's Night, 26 April 2027: the tower is lit red, white and blue with an orange lantern [U1][U17].

**Humour hooks.**
* A 14th century preacher called the tower too tall and too expensive [U1].
* Unwritten rule: nothing may be taller than the Dom [U1].
* The tower was in scaffolding from 2019 to 2024 [U1].
* The world's largest bike park, with 12,500 places [U18].
* The beer arrives by boat [U2].

## 2. Nijmegen

**Essence.** A hill town above a broad river, with two great arch bridges. On one of them, De Oversteek, 48 pairs of lamps switch on one by one at sunset [N1][N2].

**Ground and topology.** Real relief. The city sits on the south bank of the Waal at the foot of a ridge pushed up by ice age glaciers (a push moraine) [N1].
* Elevation about 29 m, ranging from 7 to 88 m [N3]. The ridge tops out above 90 m NAP [N4].
* AHN: Waalkade 13 m and Grote Markt 26.5 m NAP, so a 13 m step between the lower and upper town [N6].
* Priemstraat and Smidstraat: cobbles, 300 m, 5.3% on average, briefly over 10% [N7].
* Ubbergse Holleweg: 730 m at 7%, climbing 56 m [N8].
* Oude Holleweg in Beek: 860 m at 8.6%, steepest 300 m at 11.8%. It is called the hardest climb in the country outside South Limburg [N9][N10].
* St. Annastraat is a long drag, not a wall. On AHN it rises about 14 m over 3.6 km heading north, about 0.4% (my calculation) [N6][N12].
* The local dialect word for steep is "stikke" [N13].

**The ride.** Along the Waalkade, up cobbles into town, then over a bridge:
* the Waalbrug, with cycle paths on both sides [N14];
* De Oversteek, with a 4 m shared path [N15];
* the Snelbinder, a bike bridge hung on the side of the railway bridge, about 2 km raised above the ground to Lent [N16].

**Landmarks.**
* **De Oversteek (2013).** A single arch with a 285 m span, 60 m high [N15].
    * When the street lights come on, 48 pairs of light masts switch on pair by pair from south to north at a slow march pace [N2]. They honour 48 soldiers killed crossing the river in 1944 [N2].
    * The full sequence takes almost 12 minutes [N17]. The colour of the light is uncertain.
* **Waalbrug (1936).** 604 m long with a 244.1 m arch whose top is about 60 m above NAP. It was green until 1980 and has been light beige since [N14]. LED floodlighting in white or colour was planned (status uncertain) [N18].
* **Valkhof hill.** The 11th century Sint-Nicolaaskapel, with an eight-sided interior, is the oldest building in town. It stands where Barbarossa's castle stood [N19].
* **Stevenskerk.** Stands on its own small hill; its tower is over 70 m [N21].

**Buildings.** The upper town was bombed on 22 February 1944, so much of it is postwar. The lower town by the river is older [N1].

**Street light.** Nijmegen was the first Dutch town with electric street lighting, in 1886 [N22]. Art note: a bright riverfront below a dark wooded ridge.

**Sky.** A wide river sky. From the Oude Holleweg the flat polder (Ooijpolder) stretches to church towers near Arnhem [N10]. The Waal is the main shipping route to Germany [N23]. Art note: moving ship lights on the river.

**People.**
* Radboud University students; the university dates from 1923 [N1].
* Four Days Marches (Vierdaagse) walkers, many in uniform. On the final stretch, St. Annastraat is renamed Via Gladiola and spectators hand them gladioli [N12][N24].
* Local dialect (Nimweegs): "Nimwèège" for Nijmegen, "buute" for outside, "kieke" for look [N25].

**Sounds.** The Stevenskerk carillon, in use since 1953 [N21]. The main festival stage is on the Waalkade [N26].

**Night moments.**
* Sunset March: every evening since 19 October 2014 a veteran walks across De Oversteek in step with the lights [N2][N17].
* Four Days Marches 2027: Tuesday 20 to Friday 23 July, by the third Tuesday of July rule. Walkers start from 04:00 and on Tuesday cross the Waalbrug twice [N24].
* Vierdaagsefeesten: 17 to 23 July 2027, about 1.5 million visitors [N26][N27].

**Humour hooks.**
* "Oldest city in the Netherlands", as one of four towns claiming it. It held its 2000th birthday in 2005 [N1].
* There is a bench halfway up the Oude Holleweg where the legs give up [N9].
* The name Via Gladiola comes from the saying "death or the gladioli" [N12].
* Colour-coded march days, from Blue Tuesday to Orange Friday [N24].

## 3. Maastricht

**Essence.** On the Vrijthof square, the Romanesque Sint-Servaas church stands beside Sint-Jan with its red tower. Below it, an old stone bridge crosses the Maas, in a valley ringed by hills. In February, everyone is in costume [M1][M2][M3].

**Ground and topology.** Hilly.
* The centre is about 49 m above sea level [M1]. AHN: the Maas quay is 44 m and the Vrijthof 48 m NAP [M4]. The valley lies about 70 m below the surrounding plateaus [M1].
* Sint-Pietersberg: a flat top at 101 m NAP. Its highest point, D'n Observant (171 m), is a heap of quarry waste. The ENCI quarry beside it is a 135 ha crater [M5].
* Luikerweg, the climb out of town: on AHN it goes from 51 to 108 m NAP in 1.6 km, with about 600 m near 7% [M4].
* Cauberg (Valkenburg): sources disagree, giving 800 to 1500 m at 5 to 8%, with a maximum of 10 to 12%, up to 137 m [M6][M7].
* Keutenberg starts at 22% [M8]. Vaalserberg, the highest point of the European Netherlands, is 322.4 m [M9].
* Inside the hill are mazes of tunnels left by marl quarrying (Grotten Noord, Zonneberg), visitable only with a guide. Rembrandt's Night Watch was hidden there in 1942 [M5][M10].

**The ride.** Over the Sint Servaasbrug: limestone, 160 m long, 9 m wide, seven arches of about 12 m, with a steel lifting section at the Wyck end. Bikes and pedestrians only since 2000 [M11][M2]. Then through the Helpoort and up the hill [M12].

**Landmarks.**
* **Sint-Servaasbasiliek.** Considered the oldest standing church in the country. Romanesque, built of coal sandstone and marl, with a massive west front [M13][M1].
* **Sint-Janskerk.** Gothic, with a marl tower painted red (last repainted in 1983). It was once yellow and once white [M14][M15].
* **Sint Servaasbrug (1280 to 1298).** Called the oldest bridge in the country; its street lanterns were designed by Charles Vandenhove [M2].
* **Helpoort.** 13th century, the oldest surviving city gate in the country [M12].

**Buildings.** Local Mosan Renaissance style: horizontal bands of brick and stone, stone window frames and tall roofs. The materials are Namur stone, brick and Limburg marl [M16]. Marl is a soft, chalky limestone [M17] that cracks when wet stone freezes [M18].

**Street furniture and light.** Café terraces ring the Vrijthof [M3], and there are designer lanterns on the bridge [M2]. Other lamp types are unverified (uncertain).

**Sky.** Art note: dark hill rims above a lit valley [M1].

**People.**
* Many German and international students, with English as the common language. Locals are nicknamed "Sjeng" [M1].
* Carnival costumes ("pekskes") are homemade, with face paint and big headdresses. In the old days a bedsheet and a pillowcase had to do [M19].
* Local dialect (Mestreechs): "Goojenaovend" (good evening), "Wie is 't mèt diech?" (how are you), "Tot gaw" (bye) [M20].

**Sounds.**
* Grameer, a church bell from 1515 and one of the oldest and heaviest in the country [M21].
* Zate hermeniekes (carnival brass bands) and the Momus cannon fired at 11:11 [M19].
* André Rieu concerts on the Vrijthof [M22].

**Night moments.**
* Carnival 2027: Sunday 7 to Tuesday 9 February [M23].
    * Sunday: the cannon fires at 11:11 and the Mooswief, a doll of a market woman, is hoisted on the Vrijthof. In the afternoon the parade comes from Wyck over the Sint Servaasbrug [M19].
    * Tuesday: the brass band contest. At midnight the Mooswief comes down and carnival ends [M19].
* Magisch Maastricht, 27 November to 27 December 2026, with a Ferris wheel and ice rink on the Vrijthof [M24][M25].

**Humour hooks.**
* The band contest gives prizes for the loudest and the most out of tune [M19].
* Maastricht refuses a carnival nickname and stays "Mestreech" [M19].
* In the 17th century Sint-Servaas rang its bells wildly to drown out the sermon next door at Sint-Jan [M14].
* Herring on Ash Wednesday [M19].

## 4. Amsterdam

**Essence.** Narrow canal houses leaning over black water, bridge arches outlined in small bulbs, bikes everywhere, and the blue crown on top of the Westertoren [A1][A2][A3].

**Ground and topology.** Flat, at sea level. Wikipedia lists the city at 2 m below sea level [A4]; AHN puts the Dam and Westermarkt at 1.5 to 1.7 m NAP [A5]. There are over 100 km of canals, about 90 islands and about 1,500 bridges [A6]. The canal belt was dug from 1612 to 1682 [A7] and has been a UNESCO World Heritage site since 2010 [A6].

**The ride.** Canal quays, humpback bridges and tram rails (about 200 km of track and 500 stops) [A8]. In 2015 the city had 847,000 bikes, and bikes make 38% of trips [A9]. Tram rails that trap bike wheels are general knowledge, not verified.

**Landmarks.**
* **Westertoren.** About 87 m: a brick tower, then a sandstone stage, then two timber stages clad in lead. On top sits a blue imperial crown (blue again since 2006). It leans 88 cm out of true [A1].
* **Magere Brug.** A white painted wooden double drawbridge over the Amstel, for bikes and pedestrians only [A10][A11]. It has 1,200 bulbs that light up in the evening [A11].
* **Bridge lights.** About 80 bridges have rows of bulbs along their arches, a style a 1994 city lighting plan called specific to Amsterdam (situation as of about 2011). On the Reguliersgracht you can see seven bridges in a row [A2].
* **Oude Kerk.** The oldest building in the city (consecrated 1306), with a 70 m tower, in the middle of De Wallen [A12].
* **Houseboats.** About 2,400 households live on the city's inner waters [A13].

**Buildings.**
* Canal houses are slim, tall and deep, with a cellar and a storage loft. A hoist beam with a hook sits at the top of the gable [A14][A15][A16].
* The front door is often up a short flight of steps, for flood safety [A14].
* Fronts lean forward on purpose, about 2.75 cm per metre and up to about 30 cm per house [A3].
* Gable shapes evolved from stepped, to neck, to bell, to a flat cornice [A17].
* The story that houses are narrow because of a tax on frontage width is often told; treat it as folklore (uncertain) [A14].

**Street furniture and light.**
* The Amsterdammertje: a reddish brown bollard with the three crosses of the city coat of arms. Fewer than 40,000 were left in 2003 [A18].
* Warm bulbs on the bridges, with whiter LEDs on the Magere Brug [A2].
* Art note: the brightest of the four towns.

**Sky.** An orange grey city glow (art note); Dutch skies show few stars [A20].

**People.**
* Tall locals: Dutch men average about 1.81 m [A21]. Plenty of tourists.
* "Mokum", the city's nickname, comes from Hebrew via Yiddish and means "place" [A22].

**Sounds.** The Westertoren carillon (51 bells) plays every quarter hour, day and night. Its 7,509 kg hour bell is the heaviest in the city [A1]. Tour boats go out in the evening [A2].

**De Wallen, kept clean.**
* About 300 windows in an area of roughly 250 by 250 m [A23], lit red with black light, with curtains [A19].
* One alley is literally called Gordijnensteeg ("Curtain Alley") [A23].
* Guided tour groups have been banned since 2020 [A23].
* For the game: red neon, curtains, gawking tourists, bikes and very tall locals.

**Night moments.**
* Amsterdam Light Festival, 26 November 2026 to 17 January 2027 [A24].
* King's Day, Tuesday 27 April 2027, with parties the night before [A25].

**Humour hooks.**
* 12,000 to 15,000 bikes are fished out of the canals every year [A9].
* The houses lean on purpose, not after a night out [A3].
* Tour guides promise seven bridges; tourists count three when the bulbs are out [A2].
* Legend says two sisters built the Magere Brug so they could visit each other [A11].

## Open points

* **Lamp types:** I could not verify the street lamp types or light colours for any of the four towns. Use the Commons photos as reference.
* **Unconfirmed status:**
    * The light colour of De Oversteek's masts.
    * Whether the Waalbrug floodlighting was actually built.
    * Whether Trajectum Lumen still runs after 2018.
    * The bridge bulb details, which date from an article of about 2011.
* **Weaker sources:** the 732 wharf cellars figure comes from a blog, and the Cauberg figures differ between sources.
* **St. Annastraat:** the AHN profile shows it is a gentle drag, not a steep climb. For real Nijmegen gradients, use Smidstraat, Ubbergse Holleweg or Oude Holleweg.
* **Four Days Marches 2027 walking dates:** calculated from the third Tuesday of July rule. The city of Nijmegen confirms the festival week of 17 to 23 July 2027.

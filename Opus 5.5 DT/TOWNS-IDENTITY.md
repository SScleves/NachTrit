# Town identity: what makes each place itself at night

Backlog B1. Santi, 27 October 2026: "the cities look almost identical, that is not the plan". Every town must be
recognisable at a glance from its land, route, sky, light, buildings and people, not only from a landmark far off in
the fog. Researched with sources by four research agents; the full notes with every source link are in
`research/towns-*.md` next to this file. Bortle values are model estimates. Build notes refer to the API in
`towns3d/README.md`.

The game's levels per town keep their place (thuis, winkels, eten, station, werk, sport, mensen, eigen) and their
theme name; a town file decides what that theme looks like there.

## 1. Alblasserdam (home, tier 1)
- **Essence.** A dike village on the tidal river Noord: a superyacht yard beside a 15th century church tower. The ride to the mills starts here.
- **Ground and route.** Flat peat polder about 1.5 m below sea level, ring dike from 1277. You ride the dike top (Oost and West Kinderdijk): the river on one side, full of moving ship lights; on the other the dike houses step down into black polder.
- **Landmarks.** Oude Toren (15th century brick, small octagonal spire, a 1659 bell). The 1939 Brug over de Noord: a steel arch with a bascule span, a tide gauge on it. Oceanco shipyard halls with a navy blue superyacht hull. Container cranes of BCTN. Wipmolen De Blokker. The Kinderdijk mills on the horizon.
- **Buildings.** Low 19th century dike houses with high hipped roofs, shutters and six pane windows; a thatched farm on the dike.
- **Light and sky.** White LED street lights, no orange. Bortle 7. Rotterdam glows to the northwest, Dordrecht to the southwest.
- **People.** Bible belt, 15 churches: on Sunday women in hats. Shipyard workers.
- **Hook.** A yacht from here nearly got Rotterdam's De Hef taken apart; it left without its masts.

## 2. Kinderdijk and Dordrecht (tier 2)
- **Essence.** Nineteen mills in rows along long black water. Then the oldest city of Holland with a stubby leaning tower over three rivers.
- **Ground and route.** Polder 1.5 m below sea level; the mills lift water into high boezems. Narrow embankments between reeds and water, unlit (visitors bring a torch). Dordrecht: cobbled quays (Wolwevershaven), the iron double drawbridge Damiatebrug (1855), the Voorstraat (itself a dike), the Groothoofd where the rivers meet.
- **Landmarks.** Nederwaard: 8 round brick mills (1738), sails 28 m, sweeping low. Overwaard: 8 octagonal thatched mills in one straight line. Grote Kerk tower: 65 m of grey tuff, 2.25 m out of plumb, capped with four clock faces. Groothoofdspoort with its dome. Mill Kyck over den Dyck.
- **Light and sky.** The mill field is dark except one week a year (Verlichtingsweek, first full week of September, mills floodlit). Bortle 7.
- **People.** Millers. Dordts: "bruggie", "hé" at the end of a sentence.
- **Hooks.** The cradle with the cat that kept it level in the 1421 flood. Mill language: a sail set just past the vertical means mourning. Dordrecht's oldest church is the Nieuwkerk.

## 3. Gouda (tier 3)
- **Essence.** A Gothic town hall standing alone in the middle of a triangular Markt, red and white shutters. And cheese.
- **Ground and route.** Peat, sinking: the centre is 0.5 m below sea level. Narrow canals (Westhaven, Oosthaven) with low brick bridges, into the Markt.
- **Landmarks.** Stadhuis (1450, pale limestone, pinnacles, a Renaissance stair). Sint-Janskerk, 123 m long, the longest church in the country. The Waag (1668) with its marble relief of cheese being weighed. Round stone mill De Roode Leeuw on the Vest.
- **Light.** Warm white hanging tulip lanterns (3000 K). On Gouda bij Kaarslicht (December) the electric light goes off and the Markt is lit by candles, about 1,500 in the town hall windows, and a huge tree.
- **Sky.** Bortle 8, the brightest of the three.
- **People.** Cheese carriers and kaasmeisjes; market deals by slapping hands.
- **Hook.** "Niet te hooch niet te laech" on the inn that had to stay lower than the Waag.

## 4. Zeeland (tier 4): no town, the sea defences
- **Essence.** You ride along and over the sea defences, not through towns. Luctor et Emergo.
- **Route.** The Oosterscheldekering: 9 km of road, 65 hollow concrete pillars 30 to 40 m tall in a row beside the road, 62 steel gates between them, hydraulic cylinders above each pillar. Black water on both sides. Dark road; the island Neeltje Jans halfway is the one lit place, with the Topshuis control building, a radar tower, and the spare 66th pillar standing alone. The Zeelandbrug: 5 km, 52 identical spans, concrete barriers. The Westkappelse sea dike.
- **Ground.** At or below sea level; only the dunes rise (up to 52 m). The 1953 flood reached +4.35 m NAP.
- **Landmarks.** Westkapelle Hoog, a church tower turned lighthouse, red lantern, one flash every 3 s, its beam sweeping the fog. The Plompe Toren alone in the dike (a mermaid's curse left only the tower). Lange Jan of Middelburg (90 m) and the unfinished Dikke Toren of Zierikzee far off.
- **Buildings.** Black tarred barns with sharp white frames.
- **Light and sky.** Almost no lamps (rural posts removed). Bortle 4: many stars. Wind.
- **People.** Women's dress: lace cap, gold spirals at the temples, coral necklace, black jacket. Mussel fishermen.
- **Hook.** The national headwind cycling championship, only held at wind force 7, on the barrier.

## 5. Utrecht (tier 5)
- **Essence.** One tall tower over a canal on two levels: the wharves 2 m below the street, cellar restaurants glowing at the waterline.
- **Route.** The upper quay street of the Oudegracht with railings over the drop, brick bridges (Gaardbrug, Bakkerbrug), ramps down to the wharf. Bikes everywhere.
- **Landmarks.** Dom tower, 112 m, three stages ending in an open eight sided lantern, lit partly from inside. Domplein, where the nave fell in 1674.
- **Buildings.** Merchant houses on raised banks, cellars tunnelled under the street opening onto the wharf behind iron balustrades.
- **Light and sky.** Lit wharves over black water; city glow.
- **People.** Students on steel bikes. "Utreg".
- **Hook.** Nothing may be taller than the Dom; the beer arrives by boat.

## 6. Den Haag and Scheveningen (tier 6)
- **Essence.** Government by the sea: the Ridderzaal mirrored in the Hofvijver, then one straight road to the beach.
- **Route.** The dead straight Scheveningseweg (1665) with tram rails, to the boulevard with its levels for walkers, bikes and cars, light blue footbridges, rusted steel blocks, dunes.
- **Landmarks.** Ridderzaal (Gothic hall) and Hofvijver with its fountain. The Pier with its 50 m Ferris wheel. Kurhaus. Red twelve sided lighthouse, two flashes every 10 s. Government towers at Centraal, 130 to 146 m. De Naald.
- **Light and sky.** Bright city (Bortle 8 to 9). The orange glow of the Westland greenhouses on the southern horizon.
- **People.** Civil servants in suits with briefcases. Scheveningen women's dress: long black skirt, striped apron, pastel shawl. Red and beige trams.
- **Hook.** The capital is Amsterdam, the government is here.

## 7. Rotterdam (tier 7)
- **Essence.** Manhattan aan de Maas: a skyline of towers over a wide black river and the white swan of the Erasmusbrug.
- **Route.** Over the Erasmusbrug: 802 m, the bent 139 m pylon, a harp of lit white cables, red bike lanes hung outside the deck, tram tracks in the middle. The Maas below, ship lights.
- **Skyline (big, the point of the chapter).** Riding south: the pylon ahead; De Link with its green pixel front straight ahead; the Maastoren (165 m, dark at the foot to silver at the top) ahead left; De Rotterdam (three linked blocks) right; New Orleans, World Port Center, Montevideo with its giant M on the pier tip; the red yokes of the Willemsbrug far left; the Euromast alone like a needle far right; De Zalmhaven (215 m, the tallest in the country) behind. Tower cranes still building.
- **Buildings.** The yellow cube houses on columns, the Markthal arch, the Witte Huis.
- **Light.** White LED everywhere. The Brandgrens: 12 km of red flame lamps in the pavement where the 1940 fire stopped.
- **People.** Dockers and builders in hi-vis. Silver trams with blue and red stripes.

## 8. Urk (tier 8)
- **Essence.** A lit hill rising out of a black polder beside a black lake: a former island, a white lighthouse that turns the wrong way, a church by the water.
- **Route.** In along the lake dike past 86 wind turbines (48 in the water), up into narrow alleys (ginkies) and down to the harbour with fishing boats.
- **Ground.** The old island is a clay hill up to 9 m; the polder around it 3 to 4 m below sea level.
- **Landmarks.** Lighthouse: white, 18.5 m, copper dome on a red base, one flash every 5 s, turning counterclockwise. Kerkje aan de Zee (1786) with its onion top. Fishermen's monument: a woman looking back at the sea above 379 names. The Ommelebommelestien, a stone in the lake.
- **Buildings.** Small plastered fishermen's houses, saddle roofs, white and green fences, anchors as ornament.
- **People.** Traditional dress, worn with pride: men in wide black pleated breeches with two silver buttons, a red and white striped vest, an open jacket, a sailor's cap; women in a blue damask corset, an embroidered kraplap, a cap with a yellow lace edge over a silver head band, a striped apron, strands of garnets. Sunday rest: everything shut. Fishing boats with UK numbers.
- **Hook.** Babies come from the Ommelebommelestien. The lighthouse turns the wrong way.

## 9. Texel (tier 9)
- **Essence.** An almost empty island under a real night sky: a red lighthouse at the end of the land, sheep beside half roofed sheds.
- **Route.** Off the ferry from Den Helder, then unlit bike paths through dunes, along turf walls and over the Wadden dike. Outside the villages there are no lamp posts.
- **Ground.** Dunes to 25 m on the sea side; the Hoge Berg (15 m) with fields boxed in by turf walls; flat polders behind the dike.
- **Landmarks.** Eierland lighthouse: bright red, 35 m on a 20 m dune, two white flashes every 10 s. The white church tower of Den Hoorn. Polder mill Het Noorden.
- **Buildings.** Schapenboeten: small sheep sheds with a saddle roof cut off on the windward side. Tuunwallen: walls of stacked turf, paler than the grass.
- **Sky.** Bortle 4 to 5: the Milky Way. Aurora seen in May and October 2024 and January 2026, green and purple. Sea sparkle (glowing algae) in summer.
- **People.** Sheep farmers; more sheep than people at times. The Sommeltjes, little folk who polish the islanders' silver at night.

## 10. Groningen (tier 10)
- **Essence.** The Martinitoren over a river of bicycles, and everyone saying Moi.
- **Route.** The ring of canals (Diepenring), wide red bike paths, across the museum island from the station into town.
- **Landmarks.** Martinitoren, 97 m, sandstone, galleries and an open crown, lit warm at the foot and cool at the top. Groninger Museum on islands: a yellow tower, a silver cylinder, a pale blue block. Forum.
- **Light and sky.** Warm white hanging lamps, a wash on the facades, string lights in the trees, amber accents on bridges. Bortle 6 to 7; cloudy nights glow orange from the city.
- **People.** Students, a quarter of the city; bikes in heaps; bars open until 5.
- **Hook.** Er gaat niets boven Groningen. Peerd van Ome Loeks.

## 11. Maastricht (tier 11)
- **Essence.** The far south: a valley ringed by hills, the old stone bridge over the Maas, the red tower of Sint-Jan beside Sint-Servaas, and in February everyone in costume.
- **Ground and route.** Real hills: the valley lies 70 m below the plateaus; the Luikerweg climbs 57 m in 1.6 km (about 7 percent in parts). Over the Sint Servaasbrug (seven stone arches, designer lanterns), through the Helpoort, up cobbled streets.
- **Landmarks.** Sint-Servaas (Romanesque, massive west front), Sint-Jan with its red marl tower, Helpoort, the Sint-Pietersberg with its marl caves.
- **Buildings.** Mosan style: bands of brick and pale stone, yellow marl, slate roofs, café terraces round the Vrijthof.
- **People.** Carnival: home made costumes, face paint, huge headdresses, out of tune brass bands (zate hermeniekes). "Goojenaovend".
- **Hook.** A prize for the most out of tune band.

## 12. Nijmegen (tier 12)
- **Essence.** A hill town above a broad river, with two great arch bridges; on De Oversteek 48 pairs of lamps switch on one by one at sunset.
- **Ground and route.** Real hills: the Waalkade at 13 m, the Grote Markt at 26.5 m; cobbled climbs (Priemstraat, 5 percent, briefly over 10); the wooded ridge above 90 m. Along the Waal, up into town, over a bridge.
- **Landmarks.** De Oversteek (one arch, 285 m span, 60 m high; the lights march across it for the 48 soldiers who crossed in 1944). The Waalbrug (1936, light beige arch). The Valkhof chapel. Stevenskerk on its own hill.
- **Light.** A bright riverfront below a dark wooded ridge; barge lights moving on the river.
- **People.** Students; Vierdaagse walkers with gladioli on the Via Gladiola.

## 13. Amsterdam (tier 13)
- **Essence.** Narrow canal houses leaning over black water, bridge arches outlined in small bulbs, bikes everywhere, the blue crown on the Westertoren.
- **Route.** Canal quays, humpback bridges, tram rails. Level 129 is De Wallen: red lit windows round the Oude Kerk, kept clean and funny.
- **Landmarks.** Westertoren (87 m, blue imperial crown). Magere Brug (white wooden drawbridge, 1,200 bulbs). About 80 bridges with rows of bulbs; seven in a row on the Reguliersgracht. Houseboats. Oude Kerk.
- **Buildings.** Tall slim canal houses with hoist beams, stepped, neck and bell gables, leaning forward on purpose; front doors up a few steps.
- **Street.** Amsterdammertjes: brown red bollards with three crosses. Warm bulbs on the bridges.
- **Sky.** An orange grey city glow, the brightest of all.
- **People.** Tall locals, tourists with suitcases and phones.
